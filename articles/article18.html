WHY "DAGAPYA"?


August 30, 2026 - Published

After rebranding Dasopya, there's one question that's been hilariously absent: why name it Dagapya specifically? I expect this to be asked later and do want to write about it, so let's get into it.

When I first made the language, I named it Polupya (everything-language) since it was based on my previous attempt Taynmoga (every-community-language). This was largely a reference to how the language was supposed to be able to express everything with its limited vocabulary, but could also be interpreted as "the language that everyone speaks."

I specifically wanted to avoid naming my auxlang "world-language" or anything similar due to how common it was between auxlangs (see: Globasa, Lingwa de Planeta, Volapük, etc.). However, I was soon asked if Polupya was related to Poliespo due to their similar names, and I immediately changed it. To anyone that knows anything about Poliespo, I do not need to explain this.

In my hurry, I swapped to the closest best option, "Dasopya," which yes, means world-language. While generally happy with the name and flag, it was always one of those minor things that bothered me.

However, when I decided to do a rebrand, I realized it was a chance to overhaul everything that bothered me. That included the name, the flag, and even the genre of the language. I only really had one caveat for the new name, and that was to keep "pya," to link it to Dasopya and to keep it as a logical compound.

Before I lose the document, here's some names I considered. They were sorted into 3 categories, that being phonetic matching (names that sounded similar), reworks (names creating a new direction), and silly names that I personally thought might be fun:

dusopya (soap)
basopya (sky)
hasopya (village)
damopya (substance)
desyopya (pile)
disepya (neutral)
daysawpya (animal-time; before it was finalized to gaw)
dawsawpya (person-time)

domaypya (friend)
mukyapya (community)
sekyupya (social)
litopya (connect)
hapupya (group)
lakwepya (cute)
mimopya (candy)
lenupya (art)
balupya (categorized)

gutopya (potato; before it was changed to gupaw)
pabopya (bean)
sapapya (fish)
dabapya (banana)
botepya (blue)

Lots of choices! The ones that probably stuck out to me the most were Dusopya (funny), Hapupya (sounds nice), and Domaypya (good meaning). However, none of them really felt like they could stick.

Dusopya is only really funny if you know Dasopya, and soap doesn't link to the language itself very well. I also liked the idea of naming it something like potato-language, but then it just feels like a meme language, which it's not.

Hapupya was neat but "group-language" doesn't really mean anything? So Domaypya was my favorite, but it also looked really bad typed out, due to the glide. I would've just removed it to make the name look better, but "domay" was honestly one of my favorite words from the language.

I pored over the vocabulary, looking for anything that could mean roughly the same as "domay" while being easier to rework into the name. Eventually, I landed on Dagapya. It means "heart-language," and I changed the word for "heart" from "duga" to "daga" -- partly to make it look/sound nicer, and partly to make the pronunciation more intuitive to everyone. These are some reasons I really like the name:

1) It doesn't sound like an auxlang.

One of my primary goals with the rebrand was to move Dagapya away from primarily being an auxlang. This is for multiple reasons that could create another article, but to put it simply, I wanted a less serious community more interested in doing things like creating art.

The easiest way to do that was to give it a less serious name. "Lingwa de Planeta" cannot be interpreted as anything other than an idealistic utopian language meant to unite nations. "Dagapya: The Language of Hearts" sounds a lot more like a cutesy internet language that requires at least a little bit of whimsy to propose as an international language, which is what I wanted.

2) It relates to the language's design.

One meaning of "heart" across languages is one like "core" or "center." Dagapya centers around a small core vocabulary to try and get at the heart of every matter.

In most cases, if you want to describe something, you have to build it from the ground up -- not quite as much as toki pona, but enough that the meaning of the word is usually not obscured.

There are many cases where I thought I understood a word in English, but having to build it up out of other basic, existing words helped me understand them a lot more at a fundamental level. For example, "patience" can be translated as "annoyance-tolerance."

Seeing the vocabulary as "from the heart" can also be a figurative connection to the language basing its words off sound symbolism. These are generally consistent patterns across languages, so in a way they may speak to how our inner selves interpret meaning from sound.

3) It encourages the right kind of community.

Creating a language intended for interpersonal use means intentionally creating a community. If there was a single thing I would encourage for a community I created, it would be kindness.

This sounds really basic, and maybe a bit overly sappy, but I've been in so many auxlang spaces where kindness was not even a consideration. It's not that I need every Dagapya speaker to love each other (every language must allow hatred to be expressed), or that I want to restrict people from speaking from the heart (every language must allow honesty to be expressed), I really just want people to have kindness in their thoughts whenever they speak to each other.

In other words, I just want people to have a heart. I know they have one, anyway.
